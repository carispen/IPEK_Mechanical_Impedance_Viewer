Introduction and Terminology

This application visualizes measured and model-based mechanical impedance data, together with the corresponding phase angle of the hand–arm system, under different grip and push force conditions.

The interaction with this website is as follows:
① Conditions: For the measured data, the subject can be selected, along with the excitation direction of the induced vibration and the push and grip forces. For the model-based data, the same conditions can be set, except for the choice of subject.
② Interpolated Data: The second section displays the resulting plots for the selected subject, grip force, and push force. In total, there are three plots: mechanical impedance, phase, and vibration power absorption. A comparison of different conditions can be performed by selecting the checkbox "Compare second curve" below the plots and adjusting the constraints from ①.
③ Model Data: Similar to ②, this section contains the plots of the mechanical impedance and its phase for the conditions defined in ①.
④ Parameters: The parameters shown in this section depend on whether measured or model-based data is considered. For the measured data, the anthropometric information of the selected subject is displayed. For the model-based data, the structure of the selected hand–arm model and the corresponding component values are shown.

Term Description
Mechanical Impedance (MI) Mechanical Impedance is the frequency-dependent resistance of the hand–arm system to vibration. It is defined as the ratio between the dynamic force F(ω) applied at the hand contact surface and the resulting vibration velocity v(ω) of the hand: Zh(ω)= F(ω)/v(ω) where ω is the vibration frequency in radians per second. A higher mechanical impedance indicates that more force is required to produce a given vibration velocity. For more detailed information, refer to ISO 10068:2012.
Vibration Power Absorption (VPA) Vibration power absorption describes the amount of vibration energy absorbed by the hand–arm system during excitation. It is calculated from the mechanical impedance and represents the power dissipated by the hand–arm system as a function of vibration frequency. The frequency dependence of VPA is described in ISO 10068:2012.
Push Force Push force is the static force applied by the operator's palm to the vibrating surface during a pushing action. It describes how strongly the operator presses against the surface with the hand. For more detailed information, refer to ISO 15230-1.
Grip Force Grip force is the static force applied by the fingers when holding a handle. It describes how strongly the operator grips the handle and is balanced by opposing forces within the hand. For more detailed information, refer to ISO 15230-1.

Phase Angle of MI

What does this plot show?

The curve shows the mechanical impedance of the hand–arm system as a function of frequency.

Higher values indicate a greater dynamic resistance to the applied excitation.

Changes in grip force and push force shift the curves and may influence resonance regions.

What do the parameters mean?

The hand–arm system is represented as a four-mass oscillator model.

The masses, damping coefficients and stiffness values describe the dynamic behaviour of the system for the selected excitation direction and force combination.
